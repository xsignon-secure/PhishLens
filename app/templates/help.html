How a scan works

When you enter a domain, PhishLens performs a read-only inspection of that domain's public records — the same DNS and web records anyone on the internet can look up. Nothing is installed on your systems, no email is ever sent, and no login is required.

  • Deterministic, not AI. Every finding comes from explicit, auditable rules — the same input always produces the same result. There are no models, no guesses, and no hallucinated advice.
  • Read-only. Scans only query public DNS, mail, and web endpoints. We never attempt authentication, send test mail, or probe for vulnerabilities.
  • Exact fixes. Each failing check includes the precise DNS record to publish — copy it into your DNS provider and you're done.
  • Rescan anytime. After applying fixes, run the scan again to confirm the change and watch your grade move.

What a scan checks

A full scan evaluates 13 areas of your domain's email and web posture:

  • SPF — authorizes which servers may send mail for your domain. We validate syntax, count DNS lookups against the 10-lookup limit, and flag risky constructs like ptr mechanisms, overly broad IP ranges, and +all.
  • DKIM — cryptographic email signing. We auto-discover common selectors, fetch the public key, and verify real key strength from the RSA key itself.
  • DMARC — the policy that tells receivers what to do with mail that fails SPF/DKIM. We check enforcement (reject vs quarantine vs none), coverage percentage, aggregate reporting, subdomain policy, and alignment strictness.
  • BIMI — brand logos in the inbox. We verify the record and validate that the published logo URL serves a valid SVG.
  • MTA-STS — enforces encrypted mail delivery. We fetch the published policy file and confirm your mail servers match it.
  • TLS-RPT — reporting channel for mail-delivery TLS failures.
  • MX — your mail exchangers, including null-MX setups and misconfigurations like MX records pointing at CNAMEs.
  • SMTP TLS — whether your mail servers offer encrypted connections (STARTTLS).
  • DNS blocklists — whether your mail servers appear on major public blocklists.
  • DNSSEC — cryptographic signing of your DNS zone, which protects records from tampering.
  • CAA — restricts which certificate authorities may issue certificates for your domain.
  • TLS certificate — validity, expiry, chain, and hostname matching for your website, plus detection of deprecated TLS 1.0/1.1 support.
  • Web security headers — presence of key HTTP headers that protect visitors of your website.

How scoring works

Each check contributes a weighted portion of a 0–100 score. Critical email-authentication controls (SPF, DKIM, DMARC) carry the most weight, because they decide whether attackers can send mail as you.

GradeScoreWhat it means
A90–100Strong posture. Keep monitoring for drift.
B80–89Solid, with a few improvements worth making.
C70–79Gaps attackers can exploit — work through the action plan.
D60–69Significant exposure. Prioritize the top fixes.
F0–59Critical gaps — treat the action plan as urgent.

The action plan on every report ranks fixes by grade impact, so the first item you fix is always the one that moves your score most.

Frequently asked questions

Is scanning my domain safe? ▾
Yes. A scan only reads public DNS and web records — the same information anyone can query. It sends no email, attempts no logins, and changes nothing on your infrastructure.
Do I need an account to scan? ▾
No. The scanner on the home page is free and needs no signup. Accounts add monitoring, drift alerts, scan history, team access, and API keys.
How often should I rescan? ▾
Rescan after every DNS change to confirm it took effect. With a free account you can also schedule automatic rescans and get alerted if a score drops or a record changes unexpectedly — which can be an early sign of tampering.
Can I share a report with my team? ▾
Yes — reports are private by default, and you can generate an opt-in shareable link for any scan from your dashboard.
If my grade is A, am I safe from phishing? ▾
DNS fixes stop spoofing. They don't stop phishing. A perfect grade means attackers can't easily forge mail as your domain — but phishing still reaches your people through lookalike domains, compromised accounts, and credential theft. That's why reports with gaps also point to phishing-resistant authentication: AUTH BioPro + AMS from XSignOn — hardware-bound FIDO2 keys with on-device fingerprint, plus lifecycle management for every authenticator. What data do you keep about my scans? ▾
Anonymous one-off scans keep only the domain and result. Account scans are tied to your workspace so you get history and monitoring. You can export all your data from the dashboard at any time, and shareable report links are strictly opt-in.

For teams

  • Monitoring & alerts — watch domains continuously; get notified when a score drops, a record changes, or a certificate nears expiry.
  • Scheduled rescans — automatic re-checks on a cadence you choose.
  • Team workspaces — invite teammates with owner/member roles; every account supports authenticator-app MFA.
  • API keys — run scans programmatically with per-key prefixes and revocation.
  • SIEM webhooks — alert payloads delivered as signed JSON (HMAC-SHA256), ready for ingestion.
  • Slack, Teams & Discord alerts — paste a chat webhook URL and alerts arrive as a readable message.
  • DMARC reports — upload the aggregate reports sent to your rua= address to see every service sending as your domain, which ones fail, and when it's safe to move to p=reject.
  • Lookalike domains — find registered look-alikes of your domain (typos, swapped letters, other TLDs). Monitored domains are rechecked weekly and alert when a new one appears.
  • Header check — paste a suspicious email's headers at /tools/headers to see whether it really came from who it claims.
  • Audit log — an append-only record of security-relevant actions in your workspace.
  • Data export — download everything in your workspace as JSON, any time.
